# Project Vision

## Working Name

SIDW/BOTE is the working product direction for the AI Classroom Inquiry Assistant. In the current app, it appears as "Inquiry Classroom," a classroom AI support prototype focused on OpenSciEd 7.5 Ecosystem Dynamics & Biodiversity, Lesson 6.

The product is not meant to be a generic chatbot. It is meant to become a teacher-controlled instructional scaffolding platform that helps teachers see student needs quickly, support students with targeted prompts, manage intervention-style data, and keep AI use purposeful inside real classroom routines.

## Users

### Teachers

Teachers are the primary users. They configure lessons, define mastery, manage student profiles, control AI support levels, review misconceptions, monitor Inquiry Credit usage, moderate student questions, and decide which resources students can access.

### Students

Students use short, focused sessions to complete lesson activities, ask for guided help, submit Driving Question Board questions, and receive scaffolded support without being given direct answers.

### Parents and Guardians

Parents and guardians are a future audience. They may eventually see student progress summaries, intervention notes, teacher-approved resources, and family-friendly explanations of what students are working on. This is not built yet.

### Administrators

Administrators are a future audience. They may eventually review schoolwide support trends, intervention movement, resource use, classroom adoption, and data needed for school or district planning. This is not built yet.

## Core Problem

Teachers need fast, practical ways to understand where students are during instruction, especially when students are working through complex thinking tasks. Students need help that guides their thinking without replacing productive struggle or classroom discussion.

The app solves this by combining:

- teacher-defined mastery goals
- student profile information
- Hot List support signals
- AI scaffolding guardrails
- resource management
- misconception tracking
- short, purposeful student AI interactions

## Overall Goal

The goal is to make classroom AI usable, safe, and instructionally meaningful. The app should help teachers answer:

- Who needs support right now?
- Who is close to moving up a proficiency level?
- What misconceptions are appearing?
- Which questions are students asking?
- Which resources are available and shareable?
- How much AI support is each student receiving?
- What should happen next in the classroom?

## Classroom And School Use Case

The current prototype is built around OpenSciEd Lesson 6: "If palm oil is not going away, how can we design palm farms to support orangutans and farmers?"

In a classroom, the teacher launches a configured lesson, students work through the seven Lesson 6 activities, and the app captures student responses, questions, misconceptions, and support needs. The teacher can then open detail panels for Hot List students, students needing support, extension students, common misconceptions, question quality, Inquiry Credits, English/Spanish usage, and resources.

At the school level, the long-term use case is a shared instructional support system for intervention, enrichment, standards tracking, and resource coordination.

## Long-Term Vision

The long-term vision is a school-ready platform that helps teachers and administrators connect classroom instruction, intervention planning, student support, and ethical AI use.

Future versions should support:

- multiple lessons and subjects
- real student accounts
- parent/guardian views
- admin dashboards
- attendance, behavior, and academic monitoring
- intervention tracking
- file and resource libraries
- role-based authentication
- database-backed records
- exportable reports
- AI provider integration with strong guardrails
- downloadable or deployable app paths for schools

## Design Principles

- Simple: teachers should understand the screen quickly.
- Clickable: dashboard cards and resource items should open usable details.
- Teacher-friendly: the teacher controls lesson setup, support level, resources, and AI behavior.
- Student-centered: students receive scaffolding that supports thinking rather than answers.
- Mobile/tablet friendly: the app should work on Chromebooks, tablets, and phones.
- Privacy-aware: student data should be treated as sensitive, and public student-facing content should be moderated or anonymized.
- Demo-ready: every major area should contain seeded sample data so the product can be explained without a live backend.
- Instruction-first: AI should support classroom learning and return students to notebooks, groups, and teacher-led activity.
